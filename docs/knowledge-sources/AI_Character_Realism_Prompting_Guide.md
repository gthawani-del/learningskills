# AI Character Realism: Consolidated Prompting Guide

**Version:** 1.0  
**Prepared:** 18 October 2026  
**Purpose:** A reusable reference for writing, reviewing and improving prompts for believable human characters and photographic images.

## Scope and provenance

This document expands the 37-point consolidation from our chat into practical instructions and reusable prompt language. It covers human appearance, photography, character identity, composition and text governance.

The underlying consolidation used the available chat history and recovered details from the avatar realism discussions. Some older conversations were truncated or unavailable. This is not a verified transcript of every realism discussion. The examples, application notes and templates below are editorial expansions of the consolidated points, rather than quotations from earlier messages.

The objective is to improve the probability of a convincing result. Prompt wording alone cannot guarantee realism, correct anatomy or identical identity across generations. Review the actual output.

## How to use this guide

1. Define the image's purpose, subject, action and required framing.
2. Select the relevant realism instructions below.
3. Add reference and identity instructions when continuity is required.
4. Specify photographic treatment appropriate to the scene.
5. Remove instructions that conflict with the requested shot.
6. Review the generated image against the acceptance checklist.

Do not paste every instruction into every prompt. A close-up portrait, full-body fashion image and wide cinematic frame need different levels of detail. For example, visible pores are relevant in a close-up; they should not become exaggerated texture on a distant face.

## The three separate requirements

| Requirement | What it means | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Biological realism | The person has believable human features, anatomy and surface detail. | Waxy skin, artificial eyes, malformed fingers. |
| Photographic realism | Lighting, colour, perspective and focus resemble a coherent photograph. | CGI appearance, excessive blur, inconsistent shadows. |
| Identity consistency | The established person remains recognisable across changes. | A realistic image of a different-looking person. |

An image can succeed on one requirement and fail another. Evaluate all three when character continuity matters.

## The 37 consolidated principles

### 1. Aim for an actual photograph

Describe a concrete photographic outcome rather than relying only on labels such as “photorealistic” or “hyperreal.” Establish what is photographed, where it is photographed and how the image should behave visually.

**Prompt wording:** “A believable photograph of a real human in a physical setting, with authentic surface textures and coherent photographic lighting.”

### 2. Specify the person clearly

Define the relevant age range, ethnicity, facial structure, build, hairstyle and styling. Avoid leaving every characteristic unspecified, which can produce an interchangeable beauty-model appearance. Describe individuals without treating an ethnicity as a single facial template.

**Prompt wording:** “An Indian woman in her early thirties, with an oval face, naturally shaped eyebrows, shoulder-length dark hair and restrained professional styling.”

### 3. Preserve natural facial asymmetry

Human faces are not perfectly mirrored. Allow subtle differences in eyebrow height, eye shape, cheek structure and the corners of the mouth. Keep these differences understated and consistent with the person.

**Prompt wording:** “Subtle natural facial asymmetry; preserve the individual shape and placement of her features.”

### 4. Include believable skin texture

Skin should retain irregular pores, fine lines and small surface variations where the framing makes them visible. Detail should match the image scale and focus. Avoid turning the entire face into uniformly rough skin.

**Prompt wording:** “Natural skin texture with irregular pores and fine lines visible only at a believable photographic scale.”

### 5. Retain small skin variations

Allow subtle pigmentation differences, mild redness, minor blemishes and natural tonal unevenness. These should fit the person's age and appearance. Do not add conspicuous blemishes indiscriminately or alter identifying marks in a reference.

**Prompt wording:** “Subtle natural variation in skin tone, with restrained pigmentation differences and mild local redness.”

### 6. Include fine facial hair where visible

Peach fuzz and fine facial hairs can support a convincing close-up. Their visibility should depend on lighting and distance; they should not look like a heavy coating.

**Prompt wording:** “Very fine facial hair visible subtly where the light catches it.”

### 7. Allow realistic skin highlights

Skin can have a small amount of natural oil and local shine. Highlights should follow the light and surface shape. Avoid uniformly glossy or uniformly matte treatment across the whole face.

**Prompt wording:** “Restrained natural skin sheen, with local highlights consistent with the light source.”

### 8. Avoid plastic or waxy skin

Exclude aggressive smoothing, porcelain finishes and beauty-filter treatment when natural photography is intended. Attractive styling and makeup are compatible with realism; the underlying human surface should remain believable.

**Prompt wording:** “Preserve skin texture; no waxy finish, airbrushed smoothing or beauty-filter effect.”

### 9. Make eyes anatomically believable

Use natural iris detail, believable eye whites and realistic lashes. Subtle visible capillaries may suit a close-up, but should not be exaggerated. The direction of gaze must match the scene.

**Prompt wording:** “Natural iris detail, softly off-white sclera, subtle capillaries where visible and individually believable eyelashes.”

### 10. Keep lips natural

Preserve fine lip grooves, natural shape and believable moisture. Avoid uniformly smooth, inflated or excessively glossy lips unless those characteristics are explicitly part of the brief or reference.

**Prompt wording:** “Natural lip shape and fine lip texture, with restrained moisture and no artificial smoothing.”

### 11. Avoid perfect artificial teeth

When teeth are visible, use believable colour, shape and individual variation. Do not force an open-mouth smile just to show teeth. When editing a reference, retain the person's dental appearance.

**Prompt wording:** “If teeth are visible, retain natural tooth colour and slight individual variation; avoid a uniform brilliant-white strip.”

### 12. Render hair as real hair

Include individual strands, baby hairs and small irregularities appropriate to the hairstyle. Avoid a single solid mass, repeated strand patterns or a synthetic wig appearance.

**Prompt wording:** “Believable hair strands, subtle baby hairs and small natural irregularities around the hairline.”

### 13. Check ears, hands and nails

Realism should extend beyond the central face. Check finger count, joints, proportions, nail placement, ear structure and the relationship between hands and held objects. Request correct anatomy, then inspect the result.

**Prompt wording:** “Anatomically credible ears and visible hands, with natural finger proportions, joints and nails.”

### 14. Use restrained expressions

Direct the emotional state clearly. Neutral attention, a subtle smile and a controlled expression were relevant to our executive and avatar examples. Other scenes may require stronger emotion; the expression must fit the action.

**Prompt wording:** “An attentive, relaxed expression with a subtle natural smile.”

### 15. Keep styling appropriate to the role

Clothing, makeup, hair and posture should communicate the intended role. The customer-service executive example required credible professional presentation. Avoid automatically applying glamour styling to every person.

**Prompt wording:** “Professional customer-service styling, restrained makeup and clothing appropriate to the workplace.”

### 16. Specify the camera angle explicitly

State front-facing, three-quarter, profile, low angle or another required view. The straight-on, eye-level view discussed earlier was specific to the executive portrait. It is not a universal rule for photographic realism.

**Prompt wording:** “Straight-on portrait at eye level, with the camera facing the subject directly.”

### 17. Use face-first framing for conversational avatars

For a speaking avatar, a large head-and-shoulders composition makes the eyes, mouth and expression readable. Leave appropriate room around the head and avoid accidental cropping. Wider compositions remain appropriate for other uses.

**Prompt wording:** “Head-and-shoulders composition, with the face large enough for clear conversational expression.”

### 18. Use direct gaze when addressing the viewer

Customer-service and interactive avatars should usually look toward the camera when speaking directly to the user. A narrative character may instead look at another person or an object. Match gaze to the intended interaction.

**Prompt wording:** “Looking directly into the camera with calm, attentive eye contact.”

### 19. Choose a photographic lens description appropriate to the shot

We discussed a 70–85 mm full-frame portrait look for the executive image. Use that as portrait direction, not a mandatory setting for every scene. Lens language should support the desired perspective and composition; it does not guarantee an optically exact simulation.

**Prompt wording:** “A 70–85 mm full-frame portrait look, with natural facial proportions.”

### 20. Use believable lighting

For the corporate portrait, the selected approach was soft, diffused light with natural shadows. Other scenes can use sunlight, practical lamps or dramatic light. The person and surroundings should share a coherent lighting setup.

**Prompt wording:** “Soft, diffused corporate portrait lighting, with natural shadow transitions.”

### 21. Keep colours and skin tone natural

Avoid aggressive HDR, excessive saturation and processing that changes complexion. When using a reference, preserve skin tone while allowing believable changes caused by the scene's illumination.

**Prompt wording:** “Natural colour rendering and faithful complexion, without excessive saturation or HDR processing.”

### 22. Avoid oversharpening

Fine detail should resemble photographed detail. Exaggerated pores, sharp halos and crunchy edges can make an image look processed. More visible texture is not automatically more realistic.

**Prompt wording:** “Natural photographic detail without sharpening halos or exaggerated skin texture.”

### 23. Control background blur

Use depth of field appropriate to the composition. Avoid excessive bokeh that makes the subject appear cut out or removes all environmental context. A wide environmental frame may need much more background detail than a portrait.

**Prompt wording:** “Moderate background separation, retaining enough environmental detail to establish the setting.”

### 24. Keep avatar backgrounds simple

For face-led communication, reduce clutter and distracting objects. A simple background supports attention on the speaker. This is a usability choice for avatars, not a rule that every realistic image must have an empty background.

**Prompt wording:** “A quiet, uncluttered office background that supports attention on the speaker.”

### 25. Make gestures deliberate

When a gesture is needed, state its purpose and position. We discussed a half-body image with one naturally positioned hand as a controlled option for a conversational avatar. Do not apply a one-hand rule to scenes that require both hands.

**Prompt wording:** “Half-body framing with one relaxed hand making a small, natural conversational gesture.”

### 26. Leave hands outside the frame when unnecessary

For a face-led portrait, hands may add no useful information. Compose accordingly. For product demonstrations, action scenes and other hand-dependent images, keep the required hands visible and inspect them carefully.

**Prompt wording:** “Head-and-shoulders portrait; hands remain outside the frame.”

### 27. Keep important features unobstructed

Avoid unintended obstruction of the face, mouth or required hand gesture. Hair, props and clothing should not hide features needed for communication or identity review. Intentional occlusion can still be appropriate to a narrative shot.

**Prompt wording:** “Keep the eyes and mouth clearly visible, with no distracting obstruction.”

### 28. Preserve identity during edits

When changing clothing, background or pose, retain the reference person's facial features, appearance and skin tone. Specify which elements may change. Reference instructions improve direction but still require output comparison.

**Prompt wording:** “Use the attached person as the identity reference. Preserve facial structure, recognisable features and complexion while changing the outfit and setting as requested.”

### 29. Preserve distinctive identity marks

Maintain visible moles, scars, eyebrow shapes, ear shapes and other distinguishing features. Do not replace these with generic beauty features or invent new prominent marks in later views.

**Prompt wording:** “Retain the reference person's distinctive facial marks, eyebrow shape and ear structure.”

### 30. Create multiple reference views

Front, three-quarter, left profile, right profile and back views help document a character. Full-body views also record proportions and clothing. An unseen side generated from one reference is an interpretation until approved; it is not verified information about that person.

**Prompt wording:** “Create separate front, three-quarter, left-profile, right-profile and back views of the same character, maintaining consistent proportions and identifying features.”

### 31. Maintain identity across outfits and shots

Clothing, location and camera angle can change without changing the person. Reuse the approved reference and stable identity description. Check the hairline, face shape, body proportions and identifying marks after each change.

**Prompt wording:** “The same established character in a different outfit and location; preserve identity and body proportions.”

### 32. Judge consistency separately from realism

A beautifully rendered image can still depict the wrong person. Compare new outputs with the approved reference rather than approving them solely because they look photographic.

**Review question:** “Does this look like the same person, as well as a believable photograph?”

### 33. Exclude CGI and illustration cues when photography is intended

Avoid rendered skin, synthetic hair and illustration-like surfaces in the photographic portion of the image. If the brief intentionally combines photography with graphic elements, apply this restriction specifically to the photographed subject.

**Prompt wording:** “Photographic human appearance; no CGI skin, illustrated facial features or synthetic hair treatment.”

### 34. Keep text off unless requested

Default to TEXT OFF for photography, UGC, character sheets, cinematic frames, environments and lifestyle imagery when typography has not been requested. Do not add captions, slogans, labels, stickers or decorative pseudo-lettering.

**Prompt wording:** “TEXT OFF: do not add captions, slogans, decorative lettering, stickers or invented text.”

### 35. Do not turn empty surfaces into text opportunities

Blank walls, mugs, clothing, windows, furniture and props should not automatically acquire invented writing. An empty surface is part of the scene, not an invitation to decorate it.

**Prompt wording:** “Keep unmarked surfaces unmarked; do not invent signage or lettering on walls, clothing or props.”

### 36. Preserve authentic branding when required

When a supplied product or reference contains legitimate branding, preserve only the required existing elements. Do not invent extra labels or promotional claims. If the output distorts important lettering, treat that as a failed detail requiring correction.

**Prompt wording:** “Preserve the supplied product's existing branding only; add no new labels or claims.”

### 37. Check the output against the brief

Inspect the result rather than assuming prompt compliance. Review the face, anatomy, light, identity, framing and text. A strong prompt is a production instruction; the image remains the evidence of whether the task succeeded.

**Review question:** “Which requested properties are visibly correct, and which need correction?”

## Rules that depend on the use case

| Direction | Apply when | Do not force when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Direct gaze | A person addresses the viewer. | A character interacts with another person or object. |
| Head-and-shoulders framing | A conversational avatar needs a readable face. | The brief requires a full-body or environmental shot. |
| Soft corporate lighting | A professional executive portrait is intended. | The scene requires another credible lighting treatment. |
| 70–85 mm portrait look | Natural portrait perspective suits the brief. | Another perspective or shot scale is required. |
| Simple background | Visual focus should stay on the speaker. | The environment is essential to the story. |
| Hands outside frame | Hands add no useful information. | A gesture, action or product demonstration requires them. |
| Visible pores and peach fuzz | The face is close and sufficiently resolved. | Distance and focus would hide those details. |
| Identity continuity | The same person must recur. | The user requests a new character. |
| TEXT OFF | No typography was requested. | Specific text is an intentional deliverable. |

## Reusable prompt structure

Use this structure as an editing framework. Remove empty or irrelevant fields before generation.

```text
PURPOSE
[What the image is for and what it must communicate.]

SUBJECT AND IDENTITY
[Age range, relevant appearance, role, clothing and styling.]
[If applicable: approved identity reference and features to preserve.]

ACTION AND EXPRESSION
[What the person is doing, their expression and direction of gaze.]

COMPOSITION
[Shot size, camera angle, required visible body parts and environment.]

PHOTOGRAPHIC TREATMENT
[Perspective/lens look, lighting, colour and depth of field.]

HUMAN DETAIL
[Natural skin, eyes, lips and hair, scaled to the shot.]
[Anatomically credible visible hands, ears and body proportions.]

TEXT AND BRANDING
[TEXT OFF, preservation of supplied branding, or explicitly requested text.]

EXCLUSIONS
[A short list of relevant unwanted outcomes, without contradicting the brief.]
```

## Example: front-facing Indian customer-service executive

This example combines the earlier executive portrait directions. It creates a new person unless an identity reference is supplied.

```text
A believable photographic portrait of an Indian female customer-service
executive in her early thirties, with shoulder-length dark hair, naturally
shaped eyebrows and restrained professional makeup. She wears simple, professional office clothing appropriate to her role.

Straight-on, eye-level, head-and-shoulders composition. Her face is large
enough to read clearly. She looks directly into the camera with a calm,
attentive expression and a subtle natural smile. Hands remain outside
the frame. Keep her eyes and mouth unobstructed.

Preserve subtle facial asymmetry, natural skin texture, irregular pores
at a believable scale, mild tonal variation, fine lip grooves and subtle
baby hairs. Eyes have natural iris detail, realistic eye whites and
believable lashes. Do not exaggerate imperfections.

A 70–85 mm full-frame portrait look, soft diffused corporate lighting,
natural shadow transitions and faithful skin colour. Use a quiet office
background with moderate separation and enough detail to feel physical.

No waxy skin, beauty-filter smoothing, artificial symmetry, synthetic
hair, CGI appearance, sharpening halos, aggressive HDR or excessive bokeh.

TEXT OFF. Do not add captions, slogans, decorative lettering or invented
signage to the image or its background.
```

## Example: changing a character's outfit and setting

```text
Use the attached image as the identity reference for the same person.
Preserve facial structure, complexion, hairline, eyebrow shape, visible
identity marks and body proportions.

Change the clothing to [specified outfit] and place the person in
[specified setting], performing [specified action]. Use [shot size]
from [camera angle], with [expression and gaze direction].

Retain a believable photographic appearance, natural skin and hair detail
appropriate to the shot, and anatomically credible visible hands.
Lighting on the person should fit the new setting.

Avoid face reshaping, beautification that changes identity, waxy skin,
CGI appearance and unintended changes to identifying features.

TEXT OFF unless specific text is separately requested. Preserve only
required branding already present in the supplied reference.
```

## Reusable exclusion block

Select only exclusions relevant to the image. A negative list should not override a required style, action or composition.

```text
Avoid plastic or waxy skin, airbrushed smoothing, artificial facial
symmetry, synthetic hair, unnatural eyes, uniform brilliant-white teeth,
malformed visible hands, CGI facial treatment, sharpening halos,
exaggerated pores, aggressive HDR, excessive bokeh and invented text.
```

If a tool provides a dedicated negative-prompt field, its behaviour depends on that tool. This document does not prescribe unsupported settings or guarantee that every model interprets exclusions identically.

## Output acceptance checklist

| Check | Accept when | Correct when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purpose | The image communicates the intended role or scene. | It defaults to an unrelated glamour or generic stock image. |
| Skin | Texture and tonal variation suit the distance and light. | Skin is waxy, unnaturally uniform or excessively rough. |
| Eyes | Shape, detail and gaze are believable. | Eyes appear artificial or gaze conflicts with the action. |
| Mouth | Lips and visible teeth fit the expression naturally. | Lips are synthetic or teeth form an unnatural white strip. |
| Hair | Strands and hairline look credible. | Hair looks solid, repeated or wig-like. |
| Anatomy | Visible body parts and interactions are credible. | Fingers, joints, ears or object contact are malformed. |
| Lighting | Subject and setting share a coherent light treatment. | Highlights and shadows suggest incompatible setups. |
| Colour | Complexion and scene colour suit the brief. | Processing changes identity or creates excessive saturation. |
| Focus | Detail and blur fit the composition. | Overprocessing or excessive blur makes the person look cut out. |
| Identity | Reference features remain recognisable when continuity is required. | The output depicts a different-looking person. |
| Composition | Shot size, angle and visible features match instructions. | The model substitutes a different shot or hides key details. |
| Text | Only explicitly required text or preserved branding appears. | Invented lettering, labels or decoration appear. |

## Correction workflow

1. Identify the specific visible failure before rewriting the prompt.
2. Keep the approved identity reference and successful directions.
3. Revise the instruction responsible for the failure without adding unrelated constraints.
4. Generate a correction and inspect the failed area again.
5. Recheck identity and the rest of the composition for unintended changes.

**Example correction:** “Retain the person, pose, outfit and composition. Restore natural skin texture and remove the waxy smoothing. Keep texture subtle and appropriate to the existing shot distance.”

## Practical limitations

- Multi-angle references document and support continuity; they do not guarantee identical results.
- A newly generated unseen angle remains an interpretation until reviewed and approved.
- Camera and lens terminology provides visual direction, not proof of exact camera simulation.
- Adding more imperfections can reduce realism if their scale or intensity is implausible.
- Text restrictions should allow intentionally requested typography and required authentic branding.
- No prompt can replace visual review of anatomy, identity and compliance with the brief.

## Compact working principle

Describe a specific person in a specific photographic situation. Preserve natural human variation, keep photography coherent, maintain identity when required, and approve the result only after inspecting it.
