## Required customer journey

1. **Song and direction.** Make Upload song the music-video entry, alongside one optional request box. Reuse existing references, not a second uploader. Preserve the exact original file; decode duration and report failures honestly. A written duration remains requested until the actual source is measured. Resolve a conflict with one clear choice; never silently truncate the song. Recover existing project choices on return.
2. **Compact music-video brief.** Show a prefilled proposal: source/runtime; Narrative, Performance, Conceptual, Lyric or Hybrid; on-screen artist choice; look/mood; delivery/aspect; original-vocals policy. Use explicit request values first. Mark safe suggestions and unknowns. Budget/quality is separate from creative format. Do not force every user through empty text boxes, default English or an invented one-minute runtime.
3. **Song map and treatment.** Establish timestamped sections, vocal passages, emotional/energy changes and useful cut cues, separating measured, estimated and unknown. Canonical supplied lyrics are the text authority. Transcription is a reviewable proposal, not truth. The selected writing model develops visual treatment and narrative beats around the song; it does not replace lyrics, vocals or timing. Analysis/planning calls need their existing scoped cost permission.
4. **Artist, cast and world.** Reuse approved identity, wardrobe, locations and visual language. Show actual candidates to select when needed. An instrumental, lyric-led or no-visible-performer film does not require invented singing cast or replacement voice. The original singer remains the vocal performance unless explicitly changed.
5. **Storyboard and coverage.** Produce a visible board over the song timeline. Every shot records source in/out, purpose, section/lyric cue, pictured subject, action, framing/camera/light, references and transitions. Mark Performance, Narrative/B-roll or Lyric/graphic purpose separately from sync need. B-roll must serve the story/emotion: inserts, places, gestures or events, not unrelated attractive filler. Select and review actual keyframes before expensive motion.
6. **Timed animatic.** Play approved boards against the unchanged master, covering its complete duration. Review pacing and ending. Use proportional fitting only as a clearly identified starting draft. Shot timing becomes song-aware only after the measured/aligned map and human review; it is not established by the existing fit button.
7. **Shot-specific production.** Route by actual requirements and verified capabilities. For each shot use SYNC_REQUIRED, PERFORMANCE_NO_VISIBLE_SYNC or NO_SYNC. Readable visible singing requires verified sync; silhouettes, closed-mouth reactions, environments and cutaways generally do not. A lip-sync task binds the exact master segment, offset, approved performer and appropriate mouth-readable shot. Optional aligned vocal stems may guide sync; the original mix remains final audio. Do not retime singing footage without rebuilding/checking synchronization.
8. **Costed production and repair.** Display analysis/planning, new/reused stills, generated clips/seconds, separate or integrated sync operations/seconds, finishing, storage and retry reserve. Select suitable models by quality/capability/cost, not uniformly cheapest. Use current registry evidence; unavailable estimates remain unknown. Preserve the original submitted job and charge status. Allow the first attempt plus one diagnosed correction within authorization, then reassess; never regenerate a successful sequence to fix one shot.
9. **Edit, review and export.** Assemble performance and B-roll around the original master, with intentional chorus progression and ending. Add approved lyrics/titles separately. Review identity, motion, framing, mouth timing, sound, continuity and technical output through complete playback. Persist exact sources, versions, approvals, costs and repair history. Export and verify the actual finished file before claiming delivery.

## Next development order and acceptance

First correct the music-specific brief and master-song binding, preserving generic film/ad behavior. Then implement bounded metadata/song-map records, song-aware treatment/shot contracts and a visible storyboard with performance/B-roll/sync labels. Follow with exact audio-segment handling, approved motion/sync routes and audiovisual review. Hosted rendering and finished-output pilots remain release prerequisites.

Acceptance examples: the stated 2-minute Hinglish request must not appear as 60 seconds/English; measured song duration must control an approved complete-song timeline; original vocals must not trigger replacement speech; only visible singing incurs sync; every B-roll shot must have a musical/editorial purpose; source/lyric/timing edits must flag affected boards/performance/sync only; saved approvals and costs must survive reload. Actual paid quality, complete export and physical-device behavior require separate evidence. Adding labels or decorative workflow cards alone does not satisfy these checks.
